FINAL CAPSTONE PROJECT RECOMMENDATION & CLINICAL JUSTIFICATION
================================================================================

PROJECT: Diabetes Early Screening & Risk Prediction (Theme 1: Healthcare)
RECOMMENDED MODEL: Model 4 - Random Forest Classifier (Tuned)

1. WHY WE CHOSE RANDOM FOREST OVER THE OTHER 3 MODELS:
- Highest Test Accuracy (75.97%):
  When tested on 154 unseen patients who were completely separated from training, 
  Random Forest correctly diagnosed 117 patients, beating Logistic Regression (69.48%), 
  Polynomial Logistic (73.38%), and Decision Tree (74.03%).

- Highest Patient Recall (57.41%):
  In medicine, the worst mistake is a "False Negative" — telling a sick person they are fine 
  and sending them home without treatment. Random Forest detected 31 out of 54 diabetic patients 
  in the test set (57.41% recall), missing the fewest sick patients compared to all other models.

- Most Balanced Diagnosis (F1-Score: 0.6263):
  It achieved the highest harmonic balance between precision (avoiding false alarms) and 
  recall (catching actual diabetes).

- Reliable & Stable Across Folds (5-Fold CV: 77.85% +/- 1.42%):
  While a single Decision Tree's accuracy swung wildly depending on how the data was split 
  (dropping as low as 68.2%), the 150-tree Random Forest remained rock-solid across all 5 folds.

2. MEDICAL LOGIC & FEATURE TRANSPARENCY:
The Random Forest model does not act like an unexplainable "black box". 
Our Gini Feature Importance analysis proves that its top decision drivers are:
  1. Blood Glucose (33.3% importance)
  2. Body Mass Index / BMI (13.4% importance)
  3. Patient Age (11.9% importance)
  4. Diabetes Pedigree Function / Family History (8.8% importance)
  5. Serum Insulin (7.4% importance)

This order aligns exactly with how real-world doctors diagnose Type 2 Diabetes: 
elevated blood sugar combined with obesity and advancing age are the three most 
critical risk factors.

3. CONCLUSION FOR CLINICAL USE:
The tuned Random Forest model strikes the best balance of safety, accuracy, and interpretability. 
It is the clear and undisputed winner of this capstone evaluation and is recommended 
for deployment in the hospital screening dashboard.
